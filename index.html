Study Guide

Use this guide to learn decision rules and distinctions, not just vocabulary. CHRP-style questions often reward the best, first or most appropriate response among several plausible choices.

Strategy & HR Planning

Focus on the logic chain: business strategy → capability requirements → workforce demand → workforce supply → gap → HR response → measure. Distinguish vertical alignment from horizontal alignment. Know environmental scanning, SWOT as a framing tool, forecasting, scenario planning, succession risk, build/buy/borrow decisions and implementation measures.

Professional Practice

Prioritize integrity, confidentiality, appropriate disclosure, impartiality, documentation, stakeholder analysis and evidence. For “BEST/FIRST” questions, diagnose before prescribing. Separate activity measures from outcome measures, and distinguish leading from lagging indicators. Strong HR recommendations tie evidence to business impact and identify risk.

Organizational Effectiveness

Know organization design, centralization/decentralization, job design, culture, engagement, motivation, team dynamics, power, conflict and change. Expect scenario questions where several answers sound reasonable; select the intervention that addresses the diagnosed cause with the least unnecessary disruption.

Recruitment & Selection

Know job analysis, sourcing, yield ratios, structured interviews, work samples, reliability, validity, bias, decision rules and realistic job previews. Reliability = consistency; validity = job-relevant accuracy/prediction. Strong selection methods are standardized, job related and scored consistently.

Learning & Development

Think needs analysis → objectives → instructional design → delivery → transfer → evaluation. Do not recommend training when the gap is caused by incentives, workflow, resources or unclear expectations. Know formative vs summative evaluation and the difference between learning during training and transfer back to the job.

Total Rewards

Separate internal equity (job evaluation) from external competitiveness (market pricing) and individual positioning (range placement/compa-ratio). Know base pay, variable pay, benefits, recognition, pay compression, salary ranges and common calculations. Always read whether a question is asking about the job, the market or the individual.

Labour & Employee Relations

Know the logic of collective bargaining and dispute resolution: grievance, mediation, rights arbitration and interest arbitration. For employee-relations questions, favour fair fact-finding, consistent process, documentation, proportionate response and clear expectations rather than reflexive punishment.

Health, Wellness & Safety

Think hazard → risk → control. The hierarchy of controls generally prefers eliminating the hazard over relying on worker behaviour or PPE. For wellness/absence scenarios, distinguish prevention, accommodation/return-to-work support, attendance management and measurement.

HR Metrics, Finance & Reporting

Be comfortable interpreting ratios and translating HR activity into business terms. Know ROI, cost-benefit analysis, turnover, cost per hire, yield, headcount/FTE concepts, benchmarking and basic financial reasoning. Check numerator, denominator and time period before calculating.

Exam decision rules

  • FIRST: diagnose, verify facts, identify the requirement or root cause before jumping to a solution.
  • BEST: choose the option most directly tied to the stated objective, evidence and stakeholder/business context.
  • Selection: favour standardized, job-related, reliable and valid methods over intuition.
  • Training: do not prescribe training when the cause is resources, incentives, process, role clarity or selection.
  • Rewards: separate internal job value, external market value and the individual's location in the range.
  • Metrics: inspect the numerator, denominator and time period; distinguish activity from outcome and leading from lagging measures.
  • Safety: eliminate/control the hazard at source where feasible before relying mainly on worker behaviour or PPE.
  • Ethics: preserve integrity, confidentiality, impartiality and appropriate escalation.
